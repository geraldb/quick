A: FC Blau-Weiß Linz, commonly known as BW Linz, has an interesting history regarding its name and formation:

1997: FC Blau-Weiß Linz was founded on July 2, 1997. The club was established as a successor to the defunct FC Linz, which had merged with LASK Linz due to financial difficulties [1][2].

1997-1998: Initially, the club retained the name FC Blau-Weiß Linz and started playing in the 1. Oberösterreichische Landesliga [2].

2000-2001: The club achieved promotion to the Regionalliga Mitte, maintaining the name FC Blau-Weiß Linz [2].

2010-2011: After several seasons in the Regionalliga Mitte, the club continued to compete under the same name [2].

2022-2023: BW Linz secured promotion to the Austrian Bundesliga for the first time in their history1.
The club has consistently used the name FC Blau-Weiß Linz since its inception, honoring the traditions of its predecessor, FC Linz.

Q: naming history of Beerschot VA

A: Beerschot VA, officially known as Koninklijke Beerschot Voetbalclub Antwerpen, has an interesting naming history that reflects its evolution over the years:

1899: The original club, Beerschot AC, was founded [1].

2013: Beerschot AC faced financial difficulties and was declared bankrupt. KFCO Wilrijk, a club founded in 1921, decided to integrate the identity of Beerschot AC, adopting its colors and fan base [2].

2013-2019: The club operated under the name KFCO Beerschot Wilrijk [3].

2019: The club was renamed to K Beerschot VA (Koninklijke Beerschot Voetbalclub Antwerpen) to reflect its heritage and connection to the original Beerschot AC [3].
The club continues to play its home games at the Olympisch Stadion in Antwerp and has a strong following, known for their purple and white colors [2].

3. **WSG Wattens (1984–2019)**: After a merger with FC Wacker Innsbruck in 1971, the club retained its identity and later became WSG Wattens in 1984.
4. **WSG Swarovski Tirol (2019–2021)**: Upon promotion to the Austrian Bundesliga in 2019, the club was renamed WSG Swarovski Tirol.
5. **WSG Tirol (2021–present)**: In 2021, Swarovski ended its sponsorship, leading to the current name, WSG Tirol³⁴.

Q: naming history of FC Wacker Innsbruck (football club)

A:  FC Wacker Innsbruck has a rich and complex history with several name changes over the years:

1. **FC Wacker Innsbruck (1915–1971)**: The club was originally founded in 1915.
2. **SSW Innsbruck (1971–1986)**: In 1971, FC Wacker Innsbruck merged with SV Wattens to form SSW Innsbruck.
3. **FC Wacker Innsbruck (1986–1992)**: The club reverted to its original name in 1986.
4. **FC Tirol Innsbruck (1993–2002)**: After financial difficulties, the club was rebranded as FC Tirol Innsbruck in 1993.
5. **FC Wacker Tirol (2002–2007)**: Following the bankruptcy of FC Tirol Innsbruck, a new club was formed and named FC Wacker Tirol.
6. **FC Wacker Innsbruck (2007–present)**: In 2007, the club was renamed back to FC Wacker Innsbruck to honor its historical roots³⁴⁶.

The club has experienced various successes and challenges, including multiple championships and financial struggles.
